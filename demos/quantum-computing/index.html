Nadir Q-25

Computing begins where heat ends.

Nadir builds superconducting quantum processors and the cryogenic machines that keep them silent: six stages, from room temperature to ten millikelvin.

Request early access Scroll to descend

Pulse-tube stages, 4 K

Every stage strips away heat. And with it, noise.

A two-stage pulse-tube cooler takes the machine from 300 kelvin down to 4. Hundreds of coaxial lines are hand-formed into loops, so they survive the contraction without a single break.

Still and cold plate, 100 mK

A thousand signals, attenuated to a whisper.

Every line passes attenuators and filters at each plate, so the instruction reaches the qubit and the warmth of the room never does.

Mixing chamber, under 10 mK

Ten millikelvin. Colder than deep space.

Helium-3 dissolving into helium-4 pulls the last heat out of the mixing chamber. Deep space idles at 2.7 kelvin; this is about 270 times colder.

The processor, 9.8 mK

Where qubits hold their breath.

Twenty-five superconducting qubits, shielded from every trace of heat and light, hold a quantum state long enough to compute with it.
